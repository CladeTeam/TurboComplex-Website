    03 — Stacking the gains

    From 6,269 s to 1,501 s

    Each layer of TurboComplex compounds on the last. Boltz-2 on the full FoldBench protein–protein set (239 inputs), all on a single GPU: sampling budget, TensorRT kernels and systems engineering take end-to-end time down 4.2× — with mean DockQ moving from 0.4956 to 0.4945.

    04 — FoldBench benchmark

    Faster across seven backbones, accuracy held.

    We swept sampling steps (SS), diffusion samples (DS) and the recycling early-stop threshold (TH) — 237 full-benchmark configurations — then carried the best trade-off to every model on protein–protein and protein–DNA complexes.

    05 — Virtual screening · TC-PPI

    Genome-scale interaction screens in hours, not years.

    TC-PPI is a two-stage funnel: a structure-aware language model (SaProt, post-trained on multi-species PPI pairs) screens everything, then seven TurboComplex-accelerated structure predictors re-score the survivors through a calibrated classifier.

    06 — Binder design

    Twice the design loop, 60% more hits per GPU-hour.

    Iterative binder design calls the structure predictor hundreds of times. With TurboComplex early stopping inside Protenix v1.0, each design run costs half as much — so a fixed GPU budget buys more high-confidence binders, for protein and DNA targets alike.

    07 — Large assemblies

    Build giants one interface at a time.

    Some complexes are too big for a single shot. TurboComplex predicts subunit pairs and groups with an accelerated AlphaFold 3, then assembles them combinatorially (CombFold-style) using PAE as the ensemble score — 25 benchmark complexes of up to 28 chains.

    08 — Multi-conformation

    Ten times fewer models. Both states found.

    Proteins move. TurboComplex clusters the MSA, pre-screens clusters by confidence and only spends full sampling on the informative ones — on P21589, 37 of 370 clusters recover the same best open and closed models as the full ensemble.
